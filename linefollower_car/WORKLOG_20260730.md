## Part 1 — Why the car fails at the concrete block

### The evidence

Primary run: `logs/lf_20260730_154031.jsonl`, autopilot segment 5,
tub indices **190505–191032** (t = 191.2 s → 217.6 s). Live tuning during
the segment was **lane offset `+0.70`, curve gain `−0.20`**.

Frames are in `data_line/images/<idx>_cam_image_array_.jpg`; the failure
was replayed through the current `line_following.py` (with `myconfig.py`
and the run's *actual* live tuning applied per-frame from the telemetry)
and the replay reproduces the recorded behaviour closely, so the
conclusions below are measurements rather than inference.

### What actually happens

**The car is tracking cleanly right up to the corner.** idx 190790–190850:
yellow found on every frame, `err` within ±0.11, `lane_pos` 0.66–0.79
(comfortably inside the lane), steering gentle at ±0.3. Nothing is wrong
yet.

Then three faults compound:

#### (a) The heading reading freezes pointing the wrong way — and a safety floor commits to it

At idx **190862** `h_f` drops to **−0.724** and then never changes again:
it stays at exactly that value for the remaining **170 frames (8.5
seconds)**. Nothing updates it once the yellow is gone.

`LF_HEADING_CLIP` is `0.7`, so `hterm` sits **exactly pegged** at `−0.7`.
That trips the sharp-bend floor at `line_following.py:2199`:

```python
if abs(hterm) >= bend_frac * self.h_clip and self.sharp_bend_min > 0:
    s = 1.0 if hterm > 0 else -1.0
    cmd = s * max(s * cmd, self.sharp_bend_min)
```

`|−0.7| ≥ 0.9 × 0.7 = 0.63`, so the floor fires and forces the heading
command to **at least `LF_SHARP_BEND_MIN` (0.5) in the sign of the lean —
negative, i.e. LEFT — inside a right-hand corner.**

The white-tracking branch (`line_following.py:2771`) calls `_heading_cmd`
with the default `bend_frac=0.9`, so it is this general floor that
engages, not the more permissive `LF_WHITE_GUIDE_BEND_FRAC` path.

The floor is doing exactly what it was designed to do — *"refusing to
steer WITH a pegged heading guarantees losing the line"* — but nothing
checks that the pegged lean is still **fresh**. Here it is an 8.5-second-old
value from before the line was lost.

#### (b) The white-line fallback reads the corner as sideways drift

From idx 190868 the yellow is gone entirely (`raw=0`) and the follower is
in `white-tracking`. `white_x_f` sweeps steadily across the frame:

```
idx     190862  190868  190871  190874  190877  190880  190883
white_x_f  322     249     220     197     173     173     173
lane_pos  0.66    0.84    0.99    1.13    1.22    1.22    1.22
err       0.04   -0.07   -0.23   -0.39   -0.52   -0.60   -0.62
steer     -0.03  -0.21   -0.32   -0.36   -0.46   -0.54   -0.53
```

The boundary is moving **because the track turns**, but the follower has
no way to distinguish that from the car drifting sideways, so it reads it
as "I have drifted right" — `lane_pos` climbs past **1.0, i.e. past the
outer white line** — and steers **LEFT**, into the corner.

#### (c) Everything then freezes, and the car coasts in

At idx 190884 the white is lost too. `x_f` (−87.263), `err` (−0.616),
`steer` (−0.531) and `h_f` (−0.724) all freeze together: **2.6 seconds of
coasting at throttle 0.15 with the wheels held at half left lock.**

A brief reacquire at 190951–190954 swings `x_f` 67 → 153 and steering to
`+1.0`, then a **second freeze** — 1.8 s at `steer +0.267`, driving
straight at the block.

Only at idx **191016** does it re-detect, accepting a **336 px jump**
(dead-reckoned `−184` → detected `+245`, on a **384 px-wide frame**),
which saturates `err` to `+1.0` and slams full right lock. By that frame
the block already fills the camera view.

### Why the other runs went off track

The same two mechanisms, in every failed segment that day:

| Run / segment | Longest blind streak | Longest frozen steering | Max accepted `x_f` jump |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12:20 seg 2 | 9.8 s | 15.1 s | 239 px |
| 13:08 seg 1 | 1.5 s | 3.0 s | **442 px** |
| 13:08 seg 2 | 1.6 s | 3.0 s | **456 px** |
| 15:40 seg 1 | 4.4 s | 4.5 s (held at −0.93, near full lock) | 33 px |
| 15:40 seg 5 | 3.3 s | 2.6 s | 336 px |

**Both 13:08 segments died at the same large concrete planter on the same
right-hand corner** (idx ≈ 182559 and ≈ 183727) — confirmed by pulling the
frames, it is the same physical place.

The 442 / 456 px jumps — larger than the frame is wide — are the temporal
gate's deliberate forced-accept (`LF_TEMPORAL_MAX_REJECT`) letting a wrong
fix through after a few consecutive rejections. `KNOWN_ISSUES.md` already
identifies this as *"the DOMINANT driver of the instability"* and still
unfixed; this session's data confirms it is still dominant, and adds the
frozen-heading interaction in (a), which was not previously documented.

### The lane offset made it worse, but did not cause it

Running `+0.70` instead of the field-tuned `+0.50` put the car ~40 %
further out. Measured over segment 5, `lane_pos` was **past the outer
white line on 129 of 516 frames**. That is the smallest margin exactly
where the block is. It is a contributing factor, not the root cause —
the freeze in (a)/(c) would happen at any offset.

### Not fixed

Nothing in `line_following.py` or `myconfig.py` was changed. The three
candidate fixes, in the order the evidence supports them:

1. **Stale-heading guard.** The sharp-bend floor should not commit to a
   lean that has not been refreshed for N frames. This is the one with
   the clearest evidence and the most contained blast radius.
2. **Blind-coast behaviour.** Freezing steering *and* continuing at
   throttle 0.15 for 2–4 s is what converts a lost line into a
   collision. Straightening toward centre, or braking harder, is worth
   testing.
3. **The temporal-gate forced-accept.** Already documented as risky to
   retune blind (see `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`) — a fix wants validation on this
   specific corner, not a global tolerance change.

### Reproducing the analysis

The telemetry is one JSON object per recorded frame (see
`lf_telemetry.py`), joinable to the tub on `idx`. `lf_tools/replay.py`
replays a session through `LineFollower` with recorded timestamps; for
this analysis it was run over an index range with the run's own live
`off`/`cg` values re-applied per frame from the telemetry, writing the
follower's own overlay per frame — that is what makes the detector's
view of the scene visible rather than inferred.

## Part 2 — Dashboard changes

Both files are in the car directory and touch **no** donkeycar library
code. The dashboard attaches via `tornado`'s `add_handlers()`, which
inserts routes ahead of the application's original wildcard group, so a
route defined here shadows the stock one of the same name and everything
else is untouched. Removing the `attach_dashboard()` call reverts the car
to the stock UI exactly.

### 2a. Lane presets in the tuning UI

**Problem.** The lane-offset slider was labelled `-1 left lane · 0 center
· +1 right lane`. That is wrong: `lane_offset` is in lane-widths out from
the yellow centreline, and `±1.0` puts the car's target **on the outer
white boundary paint**, not in the lane (`line_following.py`'s
`LF_LANE_OFFSET` and `_lane_pos`: *"0.0 = on the yellow, 1.0 = on the
outer white"*). The label invited exactly the too-large offsets seen in
Part 1.

Worse, offset and curve gain are only meaningful **as a pair** — they were
field-tuned together — but the UI presented them as two independent
sliders and said nothing about which combinations were measured.

**Change.**

- Removed the misleading `-1 / +1` label.
- `dashboard.py` now defines `LANE_PRESETS`, the three field-tuned pairs,
  and serves them over `/api/tuning` along with `preset`: which one the
  live pair is currently sitting on (both halves must match, within
  `PRESET_*_TOL`).
- The page renders them as three chips, highlighting the active one and
  captioning it **"✓ these are the optimal settings for the &lt;lane&gt;"**.
  Anything else reads *"custom pair — not one of the field-tuned optima"*.
- Clicking a chip applies **both** values in a single request — applying
  them one slider at a time would leave the car briefly running a
  mismatched pair.

The measured pairs (2026-07-24 evening, on track — note the sign is
**negative**, opposite what the sim predicted):

| preset | lane offset | curve gain |
|---|---|---|
| left lane | −0.75 | −0.80 |
| center line | 0.00 | −0.80 |
| right lane | +0.50 | −0.20 |

`CURVE_GAIN_PAIRINGS` is now derived from `LANE_PRESETS` so the existing
mismatch warning and the new badge cannot drift apart. `−0.5 → −0.8` is
kept in the pairings but is *not* a named preset: it is interpolated
between two measurements, not itself measured.

Verified: `+0.50 / −0.20` → `preset: 'right'`; the crash run's
`+0.70 / −0.20` → `preset: None`.

### 2b. Lower-latency video stream

**Problem.** The stock handler
(`donkeycar/parts/web_controller/web.py`, `VideoAPI`) polls `img_arr`
every 5 ms — **200 Hz** — and re-encodes and re-sends it whether or not it
changed. The camera runs at `CAMERA_FRAMERATE` = `DRIVE_LOOP_HZ` = **20**,
so most of what goes on the wire is byte-identical duplicates. MJPEG
inside an `<img>` cannot skip stale frames — the browser decodes and
displays every one, in order — so that backlog *is* the lag.

**Change.** A `VideoAPI` in `dashboard.py` shadowing `/video`, with two
fixes aimed at latency rather than throughput:

- **De-duplicate.** Only encode and send when `img_arr` is a genuinely
  new array, so the wire rate follows the camera instead of the poll
  loop. This is the bulk of the win.
- **Never queue.** Awaiting the previous frame's flush before looking for
  the next means a slow link costs frame *rate*, not growing *delay* —
  whatever arrived while blocked is skipped, so the client always gets
  the newest frame rather than the oldest unsent one.

Measured on the Pi against a simulated 20 Hz camera at 384×216, 8 s per
sample over loopback:

| | frames/s | bandwidth |
|---|---|---|
| stock | 163.3 | 2133 KiB/s (16.7 Mbit/s) |
| this | 19.8 | 258 KiB/s (2.0 Mbit/s) |

**8.3× less data**, and the stream now tracks the camera exactly — 80
distinct frames in 4 s, none duplicated, none dropped.

Two findings worth recording because they contradict the obvious guess:

- **Do not "speed this up with cv2".** Benchmarked at 384×216 on this Pi,
  PIL (`donkeycar.utils.arr_to_binary`) takes **0.64 ms/frame** versus
  **1.22 ms** for `cv2.imencode` at equivalent quality. Encoding was never
  the bottleneck — duplicate transmission was. The stock encoder is kept.
- **Do not set the FPS cap at the camera rate.** The first version capped
  at 20 fps and measured 18.9: loop jitter puts some frames barely under
  the 50 ms bar and the cap drops a real one. `VIDEO_MAX_FPS` is now 40, a
  pure backstop — de-duplication is what actually pins the rate.

The route is only registered if `donkeycar.utils.arr_to_binary` imports
successfully; otherwise the stock stream is left in place, on the grounds
that a laggy video feed beats a broken one.

### 2c. Video aspect ratio

The `<img>` was styled `aspect-ratio: 4 / 3` while the camera produces
384×216 (**16:9**), so the feed was letterboxed into a shorter picture
than the sensor gives. Corrected to `16 / 9`.

## Testing

No automated test suite covers the dashboard. What was verified, and how:

- `dashboard.py` byte-compiles; `dashboard.html`'s inline JS is
  brace/paren/bracket balanced.
- `/api/tuning` GET and POST exercised against a live `tornado`
  application with a stub `LineFollower`, **both with and without** a
  `LaneOffsetCommander` wired — the latter matters because the commander,
  when present, owns the base values the UI reads and writes.
- Preset matching checked against the real values: the three presets, the
  crash run's `+0.70 / −0.20`, a deliberately mismatched
  `+0.50 / −0.80` (correctly produces the pairing warning and no preset),
  and the `myconfig.py` default `+0.40 / −0.20`.
- `/video` benchmarked against the stock handler under an identical
  simulated 20 Hz camera, and checked frame-by-frame for duplicates.
- `/` serves the page with the old label gone, the preset block present,
  and the corrected aspect ratio.

Not verified: nothing has been driven on the car since these changes. The
video path in particular has only been exercised over loopback, not over
the car's WiFi with a real browser attached — **that is the test that
matters for the lag fix and it has not been done.**

## Repository note

`/home/pi/mycar/` is **not** a git repository, so none of the above is
under version control. The `donkeycar` checkout at `/home/pi/donkeycar`
is (branch `madhav`), and is **untouched by this session** — `git status`

The de-duplication fix in 2b is a genuine bug fix against upstream
DonkeyCar's `VideoAPI` and applies to every DonkeyCar user, not just this
car — it is the one piece of this work that is a natural upstream pull
request. It is currently written as a car-directory override rather than
a library patch. Porting it upstream means moving the `while True` loop
in `donkeycar/parts/web_controller/web.py` to the de-duplicating form and
leaving everything else alone.
